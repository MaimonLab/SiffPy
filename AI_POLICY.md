# Policy on "generative AI", borrowed and modified from `QuPath` 

`QuPath` policy: https://github.com/qupath/qupath/blob/main/AI_POLICY.md

`SiffPy` is written by humans -- principally, me, Stephen Thornquist -- and I
am uninterested in the use of Large Language Models or other machine-learning derived
approaches to code and text generation in this code base (or any code base I
actively develop or assist in developing). The copyleft policy adopted in this project
came into place well before access to LLMs, when sharing code meant (mostly) helping
out other humans with limited resources, time, or skills. Now it means empowering
the most evil among us. Because so much of the early development of this code will remain
under copyleft licensing, it seems silly to change it now, but I can adopt this initial
policy.

Our policy in a nutshell:

- We don’t accept any AI-generated code contributions to repositories that we maintain.

- If there are exceptions in the future, we’ll specify these clearly (e.g., in a repo’s ReadMe).

- If possible, I would request that users not feed my code into their own LLM
instances. The knwoledge that our labors to do important science empower these tools is genuinely quite heartbreaking.


Our reasons are personal and considered. We hope others can respect them, even without sharing them. Here’s a non-exhaustive list of S.C.T.'s reasons ("including but not limited to", etc. etc.):

- I like writing code. Reviewing code? Not so much. And especially not reviewing the huge volume of code that can be generated with "AI".

- If we would give our brains any more ways to avoid hard thinking, we don’t trust that they’d continue to work for us at all. We’d end up poorly ‘maintaining’ a codebase we don’t understand, bereft of our own ideas.

- I strongly believe that the companies behind the largest development of LLM-derived
coding tools (namely: Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google) are actively destructive to:
science, mathematics, academic conduct, social cohesion,
principles of fairness and equality, distribution of wealth and power,
dissemination of knowledge, curation of knowledge, development of knowledge,
the environment, public trust, political stability, and so forth. Robots aren't going
to eat us or use us for labor, but humans will, and this type of centralization of cognitive
or intellectual capacity under extant political systems only hastens the ability to
subjugate the vast majority of the laboring classes. If you think that being able
to write your little widgets and code blocks faster is a worthwhile trade for the
collapse of everything most of us find valuable or important in the world, I cannot
stop you, but I can ask you to respect my request not to do it with projects I help manage.
I recognize that the very technology to which I object can only survive under a predatory
inclination to disrespect individual intellectual property and any sense of human
community, but I hope to appeal to the lingering humanist impulses of those who
might read be considering the use of this software for their own intellectual endeavors.

- I always hope to be responsible in the tools and datasets I use, even when it puts me at 
a disadvantage (e.g., avoiding dependencies or data that aren’t under open licenses). I’d rather not ignore our misgivings about the legal and ethical uncertainties around AI simply because it’s widespread or can generate some code blocks faster than me.

We might change. So might this policy. But, for now, we're operating under the self-imposed constraint not to introduce AI-generated code into our open source projects.
